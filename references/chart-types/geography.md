# Geographic Displays

Use maps when location is part of the analytical question, not merely as decoration. Choose a geographic scale suited to the data, identify the measure and time period, and avoid area or color encodings that exaggerate differences. Use a sorted chart when exact comparisons matter more than location.

| Chart type | Use when | Avoid or check |
|---|---|---|
| Map | Showing geographic distribution or location-based comparisons | Choose an appropriate geographic scale; include units and avoid misleading area comparisons |

## Choose a map encoding

- Use a choropleth (areas shaded by value) for comparable rates or proportions across geographic units. Avoid mapping raw totals when unit populations or areas differ substantially; explain the measure, denominator, time period, classification, and treatment of missing areas.
- Use proportional symbols when mapped counts or point locations matter. Make symbol sizing consistent and avoid overlap that hides locations. If precise ranking is the main question, a sorted bar chart is usually easier to compare.
- Choose boundaries and geographic level that match the question. Include a readable legend and source, and make clear whether a map is illustrative or uses a defined geographic scale.
