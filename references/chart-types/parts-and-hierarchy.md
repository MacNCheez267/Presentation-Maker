# Part-to-Whole and Hierarchy

Use these charts to explain how components relate to a total or how categories nest inside one another. Confirm that parts form a meaningful whole, keep the number of visible categories manageable, and label the denominator and hierarchy clearly.

| Chart type | Use when | Avoid or check |
|---|---|---|
| Pie or donut | Showing a few parts of one meaningful whole | Confirm values sum to a whole; label directly; avoid many small slices |
| Stacked bar | Comparing composition across a small number of groups | Keep category order and colors consistent; show totals when they vary |
| Treemap or hierarchy chart | Showing nested categories or part-to-whole hierarchy | Keep levels and labels simple; confirm the hierarchy is meaningful |

## Selection cues

- Use a pie or donut only for a small number of mutually exclusive parts that add to one total at the same point in time. Direct labels or a companion table help when slice sizes are close; use a sorted bar chart when accurate ranking matters.
- Use a stacked bar to compare composition across a small number of groups. A 100% stacked chart emphasizes proportions but hides differences in total size; show totals separately when those differences matter.
- Use a treemap only when the hierarchy and relative sizes are both meaningful. It is poor for precise comparison of small areas; use a labeled tree, table, or bar chart when readers need exact values or an easy parent-child trace.
