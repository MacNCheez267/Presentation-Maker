# Chart Quality and Platform Checks

Use a chart only when it answers the audience's question more clearly than a sentence, table, or labeled diagram. For every chart:

- State the takeaway in the title or nearby text.
- Show units, time period, source, and definitions needed for interpretation.
- Keep scales consistent across comparable charts. Do not crop or alter axes to exaggerate differences; use a zero baseline for bar magnitude comparisons unless a clearly disclosed analytical reason requires otherwise.
- Make labels legible at presentation size. Use direct value labels where practical.
- Encode categories consistently and support color with labels, shapes, or patterns. Use color with enough contrast against the background.
- Disclose calculations, aggregation, missing data, and caveats that affect interpretation. Do not fabricate or silently transform data.
- Verify the selected platform supports the chart type and confirm native editability before claiming the chart is editable. If unsupported, choose a faithful available alternative or label the output as a static illustration.

Use the chart-specific guide for the selection criteria and pitfalls that are unique to that visualization.
