# Matching a Style to a Brief

Start with purpose, audience, delivery context, and explicit brand or design requirements. These outweigh generic category defaults. For broad words such as “modern” or “premium,” translate the label into observable choices and state that interpretation briefly. Infer reversible details from a concise brief rather than delaying for a complete design questionnaire.

| Requested look or context | Suitable direction | Keep in mind |
|---|---|---|
| Clear, modern, general business | Product-led minimal or corporate | Use evidence and structure; avoid generic gradients as the whole identity |
| Premium, refined | Editorial or luxury | Use fewer colors, disciplined typography, and high-quality imagery |
| Friendly, reassuring | Human wellness | Keep contrast strong and avoid childish visual cues |
| Technical, analytical | Technical / data-forward or Swiss / typographic | Label diagrams and metrics; use mono type as an accent, not for long copy |
| Energetic, consumer-facing | Playful / colorful | Limit the palette and preserve a stable layout rhythm |
| Story-led, emotional | Editorial or immersive / cinematic | Let imagery support the argument; keep key claims readable |
| Handmade, local, food-focused | Tactile / crafted | Use texture as a background detail, not as a substitute for hierarchy |
| Bold, cultural, expressive | Retro / experimental | Preserve readable contrast and consistent slide navigation |

## Decide and combine

Choose one dominant visual direction and at most one restrained supporting influence. Describe concrete features such as grid, spacing, image treatment, shape language, and hierarchy rather than relying on style names alone. Select color palette and typography independently using their own references. Preserve supplied brand assets. If style conflicts with readability or accessibility, preserve the intended mood while changing the treatment and briefly note any material trade-off. Review real examples only when they help resolve a meaningful design decision; adapt qualities, not layouts or identities.
