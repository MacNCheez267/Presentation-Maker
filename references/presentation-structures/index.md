# Presentation Structures

Choose a story pattern from the presentation purpose, audience question, and desired outcome. These patterns are adaptable starting points, not mandatory sequences. For a product walkthrough, use the product-specific [Reasoning Rules](../reasoning-rules.md) only when relevant; it does not replace a lesson, research, proposal, or general-deck structure.

- [Lesson and explainer](lesson.md): teach a concept or skill.
- [Data presentation](data-presentation.md): interpret evidence and implications.
- [Product design or concept review](product-design.md): evaluate a design and decide next steps.
- [Proposal or pitch](proposal.md): support a recommendation or decision.
- [Research and findings](research-and-findings.md): present methods, evidence, and limits.
- [Strategy and executive updates](strategy-and-updates.md): communicate status, choices, and decisions.
- [Training and workshops](training-and-workshops.md): teach through demonstration and practice.
- [Launch, case study, portfolio, and advocacy](launch-case-study-and-advocacy.md): structure a product experience, documented body of work, or cause.
- [Brand and visual identity](brand-and-visual-identity.md): explain an identity system and its applications.

## Use and adapt a pattern

Treat the sequence as a planning aid, not a mandatory slide count or template. Adapt it to the supplied material, delivery time, audience knowledge, and requested outcome. Preserve required sections or sequencing; clarify a material conflict instead of silently changing it. Give each slide one primary job and vary composition and pacing to fit the content. Close with a takeaway or a meaningful next step; add a call to action only when the audience has a useful action to take.
