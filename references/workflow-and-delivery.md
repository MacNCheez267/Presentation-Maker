# Workflow and Delivery

Use the inputs that matter for the requested mode. This is a flexible path to a reviewable result, not a questionnaire or mandatory approval sequence. Follow the [platform guide](platforms/canva.md) only when Canva-specific behavior is relevant.

## Workflow at a glance

1. Identify the mode and scope. For Template, Edit, and Review, locate the exact source presentation first.
2. Establish the audience, purpose, intended outcome, and delivery context. Ask only about missing details that could materially change the content or result; use sensible defaults for reversible choices and state consequential assumptions.
3. Select the requested platform when available. Otherwise choose a suitable available presentation tool or file workflow. Inspect the current tool descriptions and follow their permissions, approval, continuation, and output requirements. If the needed capability is unavailable, explain the limit and offer a compatible alternative when appropriate.
4. Gather supplied content and identify claims that require verification. Preserve supplied material and use [Research and Sourcing](research-and-sourcing.md) for external facts.
5. Plan a story suited to the purpose. Use [Presentation Structures](presentation-structures/index.md) for general presentations and [Reasoning Rules](reasoning-rules.md) only for product- or service-focused walkthroughs.
6. In Create mode, use [Design System Generation](design-system-generation.md) and [Design Direction](design-direction.md) to select story, style, palette, typography, visuals, charts, and effects as distinct but compatible choices.
7. Create, template, edit, or review with the selected available workflow. Preserve source-design boundaries and follow tool-required approvals before committing.
8. Inspect the preview or readable content, complete the applicable checks below, and report only results confirmed by the available tools.

## Mode-specific inputs

| Mode | Identify or clarify when material |
|---|---|
| Create | Topic or source content, audience, intended outcome, delivery context, requested length or speaking time, and required claims or sections |
| Template | Whether the source is a reusable brand template or an existing presentation, its exact name or link, and whether the user wants a copy or edits to that source |
| Edit | Exact presentation, requested changes, content to preserve or replace, and boundaries on layout or brand changes |
| Review | Exact presentation and requested review lens; if none is given, review story, content fidelity, readability, and visual consistency at a high level |

Language, accessibility needs, brand assets, presenter notes, aspect ratio, and export format may also matter. Ask only when the choice materially affects the result or the user has requested it. Do not ask for details already supplied or clearly implied.

## Story and outline decisions

Choose a story that helps the audience understand, feel, or do what the user intends. Give the presentation a clear opening, an ordered sequence in which each section supports the next, and a closing that reinforces the takeaway. Use transitions to make relationships clear; vary examples, visuals, and pacing to fit the material. Do not add sections merely to complete a template or force a call to action where none is useful.

For a short, clear request, an internal story plan is enough; proceed without showing a separate outline. For a long, complex, source-heavy, or high-stakes deck, prepare a concise outline before authoring when it would catch costly structural mistakes. Include each slide's working title, main takeaway, and needed evidence or visual. Show the outline for approval when the user asks for that step or when an unresolved choice would materially change the story. Otherwise, state a consequential assumption and continue.

A clear Create or Edit request authorizes work within the requested scope. Follow any approval required by the selected tool before committing. Get separate direction before publishing, sharing, changing access, or making other actions beyond the requested scope.

## Content, sources, and visual direction

Use supplied source content faithfully. Do not invent names, figures, quotations, findings, citations, claims, or brand assets. When external facts materially affect the presentation, follow [Research and Sourcing](research-and-sourcing.md): prefer current primary sources, verify time-sensitive claims, and cite material facts near the claim or in notes. Use APA 7 when the user requests it; otherwise use clear, consistent citations and a readable source list when needed. If a claim cannot be verified and uncertainty matters, explain the gap and ask whether to proceed with supplied material only or pause for sources.

Use the user's brand kit, assets, and design constraints first. For Create mode, use [Design Direction](design-direction.md) to find and reconcile story, style, palette, typography, and effects. Treat the domain references as candidate guidance. Verify that selected fonts, chart types, layouts, and effects are supported in the chosen platform before claiming availability or application. Use [Style Recommendations](style-recommendations/index.md) for general presentations and [Product Styles](product-styles/index.md) only for product- or service-focused presentations; use [Color Palettes](color-palettes/index.md) and [Font and Typography Pairings](font-and-typography-pairings/index.md) for independent design decisions. Online examples may inform visual direction; adapt observable qualities rather than copying a layout or identity, and cite a specific example when it materially supports the recommendation.

## Production and source boundaries

Follow the selected platform's current tool descriptions, availability, permissions, required approvals, continuation instructions, and limits. Use platform-specific guidance only when it applies. If a needed capability is unavailable, say so and choose a supported alternative only when it preserves the user's intent.

For charts, follow [Chart Types](chart-types/index.md): choose a practical chart for the question, keep scales and units accurate, make the takeaway and labels clear, use color with non-color cues, and include source details. For readability, accessibility, interaction, and motion, follow [UX Guidelines](design-guideline.md). For asynchronous operations, follow the current tool's wait instructions; do not duplicate polling when the tool UI is already handling it.

For Template mode, preserve the source design unless the user asks to edit it; use a copy when a separate presentation is requested. For Edit mode, change only what the user requested and preview the draft when supported. Do not publish, share, or change access unless requested. For Review mode, inspect without editing; if available tools do not expose enough content, report only what can be confirmed.

## Review-only reports

Keep Review mode read-only. Organize findings by priority and cite the slide number or visible element when possible. For each finding, explain the issue, its effect on the audience, and a practical correction. Separate factual or narrative problems from visual polish. Do not report a defect in content the available preview did not expose.

## Final checks

Before reporting completion, inspect the available preview or readable content and check:

- The deck serves the stated audience and outcome, follows the intended story, and has no obvious gaps or unnecessary repetition.
- Claims match supplied or verified sources; quotations, names, dates, units, calculations, citations, chart labels, and source notes are accurate.
- Text is readable at normal presentation size; check clipping, wrapping, alignment, contrast, image crops, and consistency across slides.
- Charts and diagrams communicate the intended comparison or sequence with accurate scales, clear units, legible labels, and source details.
- Selected fonts, chart types, and effects are supported by the chosen platform, and the design follows the requested brand and style while keeping meaningful content accessible.
- Motion, navigation, and interactive elements are purposeful and understandable; report checks that the preview could not support.

Use [UX Guidelines](design-guideline.md) and [Chart Types](chart-types/index.md) for relevant visual checks. If the tool provides only a partial preview, state which checks you could not perform.

## Delivery

Report only what the selected tool or output confirms. For Create mode, provide the confirmed design link or file when available, a concise summary, the relevant Design Direction fields from [Design Direction](design-direction.md), and the pre-delivery checklist with passed, needs-attention, or unverified status. For other modes, summarize completed requested edits, return prioritized findings for Review, and report material limitations briefly. Do not imply an edit, review, or creation succeeded unless the available evidence confirms it.
